A token predictor trained on vast data has taken the world by storm. Many of us now rely on AI to make day-to-day decisions, offloading critical thinking to it and making use of the freed up time by consuming content online. I had thought of writing about this content echo chamber when I noticed AI-generated content in newspapers, blogs, and social media posts. I realized that most of the stuff I read online(and soon I’d watch as well) is created by a LLM. 

A paper I read recently, which also got the best paper prize at NeurIPS’25, titled ‘Artificial Hivemind’ sparked my interest in AI creativity. It shows mode collapse(convergence) in LLM answers on open-ended questions. This implies homogenization of human thought, becoming reliant on AI content, due to generation of similar answers across different models. It points to a saturation point, lack of diversity in ideas essential for creativity. 

It is also worth mentioning the societal impact of this convergence of thought. Someone who uses AI as a tool (as it should be) would still be affected as people around them begin to form beliefs on LLM content. Belief offloading is a disempowerment pattern in which humans abandon the labour of judgement and let the AI form concise knowledge which they then fit into their worldview as-is. Someone at the dinner table may blurt out a swallowed as-is LLM thought and regurgitate it to others who will be susceptible to accepting it given their human bond with the sharer. And, since LLMs collapse (converge) to a set of responses, it is quite probable that such a boring, homogenous society would be nothing but a representation of its (training) data.   

A counter to this would be: If a model is trained on the collective human knowledge is it not as good as if not better than humans at generating new knowledge? 
Although it is a hard pill to swallow, AI today is better than an average human in almost all cognitive tasks, in some sense AGI has been achieved.
Humans on the other hand, are good at inventing stuff not because they hold knowledge but because they bring in unique perspectives by looking at problems from their domain of expertise. An example would be an ornithologist (a bird expert) who looks at problems in aerodynamics from his unique point of view. This remote cross-domain mapping leads to novel solutions, something that would take AI long to mimic taking into account almost 8 billion perspectives and no ready data that captures these views. What makes humans as a whole more creative is that we are a sum of our parts and all the parts are unique. 

This brings us to think how LLMs should be used? Delegation of cognitive tasks(not restricted to repetitive) is bound to happen since that is the advantage AI gives us. As the rise of search engines reduced the need to remember ( or physically store) information, the rise of LLMs should take over implementation of ideas. However, letting the LLM decide the story makes one lose the plot.
